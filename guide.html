Staying safe from scams

Simple habits that protect you and the people you care about. No technical knowledge needed.

Four rules that stop most scams

⏸️ PauseScammers rush you. Real banks, shops and offices can wait while you check.
🔒 Never share codesNobody legitimate will ask for your password, PIN or the one-time code sent to your phone.
📞 Call back yourselfHang up and use the number on your card or the official website, not the one in the message.
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Ask someoneShow a family member or friend before you click, pay or reply.

Already clicked, replied or paid?

It happens to careful people every day. Acting quickly makes a real difference.

  1. Stop all contact. Don’t reply, call back or send anything more.
  2. Call your bank or card company using the number on your card. Ask them to stop or reverse payments.
  3. Change your passwords, starting with your email and your bank. Use a different device if you think your computer is affected.
  4. Turn on two-step verification for your important accounts.
  5. Keep evidence: screenshots, phone numbers, web addresses and dates.
  6. Report it to your national fraud or police reporting service and to the website or app where it happened.
  7. Tell someone you trust. Being tricked is not your fault, and talking about it helps stop others being caught.

Scams by situation

📱 Text messages
  • Don’t tap links in texts you weren’t expecting, even if they look like your bank, a courier or a toll service.
  • Open the company’s official app or type its website yourself to check.
  • Don’t reply, even to say stop. Block the number and delete the message.
📞 Phone calls
  • Caller ID can be faked, so a number that looks official proves nothing.
  • Real agencies don’t demand gift cards, crypto or urgent transfers, and don’t threaten arrest.
  • Never give anyone remote access to your computer or phone.
  • If a “relative” calls in trouble, hang up and ring them on their usual number. Agree a family code word in advance.
✉️ Emails
  • Check the sender’s full address, not just the display name.
  • Be wary of urgent threats such as “your account will close today”.
  • Don’t open attachments you didn’t expect.
  • Go to the website by typing it yourself instead of clicking the link.
🔗 Websites and online shopping
  • A padlock only means the connection is private. Scam sites can have one too.
  • Check the spelling of the address, and be careful with prices that are far too good to be true.
  • Pay by card or a protected payment method, never by bank transfer, gift card or crypto to a stranger.
  • For a shop you’ve never heard of, search for independent reviews first.
💬 Romance, investment and job offers
  • Never send money to someone you haven’t met in person, however close you feel.
  • “Guaranteed” high returns are a warning sign.
  • A real job never asks you to pay a fee or buy equipment before you start.

🔐 Keep your accounts safe

  • Use a different password for every important account. A password manager makes this easy.
  • Turn on two-step verification, preferably with an authenticator app.
  • Keep your phone and computer updated.
  • Check if your email appeared in a known data breach at haveibeenpwned.com.

💙 Look out for your family

  • Agree a family code word for emergency calls.
  • Make a house rule: no money moves until you’ve phoned back on a known number.
  • Help older relatives set up call blocking and two-step verification.
  • Be kind, not critical. People who feel judged hide scams instead of reporting them.
